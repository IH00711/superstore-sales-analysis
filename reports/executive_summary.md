# Executive Summary: Superstore Sales and Customers

## Objective
Find out where a US office-supply retailer makes and loses money, and which customers matter most, using 9,994 order rows from 2014-2017.

## Key Metrics
| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total sales | $2,297,201 |
| Total profit | $286,397 |
| Profit margin | 12.5% |
| Number of orders | 5,009 |
| Average order value | $459 |

## Key Findings
1. Deep discounts cause most of the losses. 97% of rows discounted 30% or more lost money, about $135k in total, which is almost half of total profit. Only 6% of rows discounted 20% or less lost money.
2. Furniture sells but doesn't earn. It has $742k in sales (32% of the total) but only $18k in profit, a 2.5% margin compared with 17.4% for Technology. Tables (-$17.7k) and Bookcases (-$3.5k) lose money.
3. A small group of customers brings in most of the profit. The top 10% of customers (79 people) generate 31% of sales and 43% of profit. The 80 "Big Spenders" (26% of sales) haven't ordered in about 170 days on average.
4. Sales are growing and seasonal. Sales rose 51% from 2014 to 2017, and September, November and December are the busiest months every year.

## Recommendations
1. Cap discounts at 20%, with manager approval needed above that. This could recover most of the $135k lost on deep-discount rows.
2. Review Furniture pricing, starting with Tables and Bookcases, and stop discounting them heavily. Bringing Furniture up to the company's 12.5% margin would add about $18k a year in profit ($74k over the four years).
3. Start a retention programme. Contact the 80 Big Spenders and 104 regulars who have stopped ordering, and give the 106 Champions loyalty perks. Together these groups bring in 43% of profit.
4. Plan stock and marketing around the September to December peak.

## Limitations and Next Steps
- The data ends in December 2017, so the trends need checking against newer data.
- There's no cost breakdown (shipping, product cost, marketing), so it isn't clear why Furniture margins are low. Product-level costs would be the next thing to get.
- Some discounts may have been given for a reason, such as clearing old stock or winning a big client. The 20% cap should be tested in one region first.
- The segments rank customers against each other, not against fixed targets, so they should be refreshed every quarter.
